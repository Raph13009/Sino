# CIVEP Design System

## 1. Brand Direction

CIVEP should feel like a serious China–Europe industrial advisory and commercial expansion firm.

The visual identity combines:

- the authority and restraint of a premium strategy consultancy
- the credibility and precision of an industrial operator
- the editorial sophistication of a contemporary international business brand
- the pragmatism of a company focused on execution, not theory

The brand must never feel like a SaaS startup, AI agency, financial institution, or luxury consultancy.

### Core brand attributes

- Premium
- Industrial
- International
- Credible
- Pragmatic
- Modern
- Sophisticated
- Commercially focused
- Precise

### Visual principle

**Consulting credibility. Industrial legitimacy. Commercial execution.**

The system should feel confident and controlled rather than flashy.

---

# 2. Visual DNA

The preferred visual direction is built from five pillars:

1. Strong editorial typography
2. Large-scale industrial photography
3. Warm neutral backgrounds
4. Charcoal / deep ink as the core brand color
5. A restrained oxide-red accent

The overall result should feel closer to a modern industrial advisory firm than a technology startup.

Avoid:

- gradients as a core visual device
- purple / blue SaaS palettes
- excessive cards
- pill-shaped UI
- large rounded containers
- crypto / futuristic visual language
- neon accents
- glossy 3D renders
- generic corporate stock photography
- overuse of traditional consulting blue
- Chinese / European clichés such as flags, stars, dragons, Great Wall imagery

- primary typography
- navigation

Primary accent.

`#FBFAF7`

`#E8E4DC`

`#C9C5BD`

Border tone on dark backgrounds.

---

# 4. Color Usage Ratio

Recommended balance:

- 55% Warm Ivory / Warm White
- 25% Deep Ink
- 10% industrial photography
- 7% secondary neutrals
- 3% Oxide Red

Oxide Red should feel intentional and rare.

Its scarcity increases its impact.

# 5. Typography

## Latin Typography

### Primary Heading Font

Preferred:
**Neue Haas Grotesk**

Alternatives:
- Söhne
- Graphik
- Suisse Intl

The heading font must feel:
- authoritative
- editorial
- contemporary
- neutral enough for consulting
- strong enough for industrial contexts

### Body Font

Preferred:
**Inter**

Alternative:
**Suisse Intl**

Inter is recommended for digital execution due to readability and strong multilingual support.

---

## Simplified Chinese Typography

Recommended:

### Headings
**Noto Sans SC Medium**

### Body
**Noto Sans SC Regular**

Alternative:
**Source Han Sans SC / 思源黑体**

Avoid mixing a highly stylized Latin font with an ornate Chinese font.

The bilingual system should remain modern, neutral and highly readable.

# 8. Typography Rules

- Prefer short, strong headings.
- Avoid centered text for most content.
- Use left alignment as the default.
- Limit text width to preserve editorial quality.
- Long body copy should generally remain under 70 characters per line.
- Use uppercase only for small technical labels and navigation metadata.
- Avoid excessive italic usage.
- Avoid decorative typography.

---

## Grid

12-column grid

`1280px–1360px`

Page horizontal padding:

- Tablet: 32 px
- Mobile: 20–24 px

Use asymmetric compositions where appropriate.

Example:

- 5 columns text
- 1 column spacing
- 6 columns imagery

# 11. Border System

Borders should structure information rather than decorate it.

### Default border
`1px solid #C9C5BD`

### Dark border
`1px solid #454A50`

### Radius

Default:
`0–4px`

Maximum:
`6px`

Where possible, prefer:
- rules
- dividers
- column boundaries
- open layouts

over enclosed cards.

# 14. Photography Direction

Photography is one of the most important brand elements.

## Subjects

Prioritize:

- cranes
- industrial machinery
- manufacturing plants
- assembly lines
- European ports
- Chinese industrial zones
- logistics infrastructure
- containers
- heavy equipment
- engineers on-site
- inspections
- factory interiors
- industrial aerial photography

---

## Treatment

Photography should feel:

- documentary
- credible
- cinematic but not dramatic
- slightly desaturated
- high contrast
- naturally industrial
- grounded in real environments

Avoid:
- fake office scenes
- staged handshakes
- smiling teams around laptops
- generic skyscrapers
- abstract digital globes
- AI-generated futuristic factories
- excessive overlays

---

# 15. Image Composition

Preferred compositions:

- large full-width imagery
- vertical crops
- extreme machinery detail
- aerial industrial geometry
- subjects placed inside real environments
- text and photography sharing the grid

Photography should often occupy 40–60% of the viewport.

Do not trap every image inside a card.

---

# 16. Iconography

Iconography should be sparse.

Preferred style:

- monoline
- geometric
- 1.5 px stroke
- technical
- functional
- consistent optical size

Where possible, replace icons with:

- arrows
- numbers
- coordinates
- typography
- simple diagrams
- directional markers

Example service markers:

`01 — Sales Coaching`

`02 — Local Intermediary`

`03 — Sales Outsourcing`

Icons should support the content, not create the identity.

---

# 17. Service Presentation

Avoid four large rounded feature cards.

Preferred structure:

```
01
Sales Coaching

Build the commercial methods, messaging and sales discipline required to sell effectively in European markets.

→ Learn more
```

Use horizontal rules between services.

Alternative:

2-column editorial grid with large numbers and short descriptions.

# 21. Content Style

The brand voice should be:

- clear
- precise
- pragmatic
- direct
- internationally understandable
- commercially focused

Avoid:

- startup buzzwords
- exaggerated claims
- vague transformation language
- AI jargon
- excessive strategy vocabulary

Prefer:

“Build a European sales operation.”

over:

“Unlock transformative cross-border growth opportunities.”

---

# 22. Motion Principles

Motion should feel controlled and almost invisible.

## Allowed

- 150–350 ms interface transitions
- subtle opacity reveals
- 12–20 px vertical entrance
- line drawing
- image mask reveals
- slow parallax on large photography
- arrows translating 3–4 px
- understated hover states

## Avoid

- spring physics
- bouncing elements
- large-scale zooms
- rotating cards
- infinite animation loops
- animated gradients
- exaggerated cursor effects

Motion must reinforce precision.

---

# 23. Editorial Details

Use small technical details to differentiate the identity.

Examples:

`01 / SERVICE`

`EUROPEAN MARKET ENTRY`

`PARIS — SHANGHAI`

`INDUSTRIAL EXPANSION`

`EST. 2026`

These can sit in margins, image captions or section headers.

Use sparingly.

---

# 24. UI Component Philosophy

Before creating a container, ask whether structure can be communicated through:

- whitespace
- typography
- alignment
- rules
- image composition

Cards should be the exception.

Do not create a dashboard-like website.

The site is a corporate advisory platform.

---

# 25. Responsive Principles

On mobile:

- preserve strong typography
- stack asymmetric grids cleanly
- maintain generous vertical rhythm
- keep photography large
- avoid shrinking desktop layouts into dense components
- convert multi-column service structures into separated rows

Premium perception must remain on small screens.

---

# 26. Accessibility

Minimum body contrast:
WCAG AA

Minimum body size:
16 px

Avoid text directly over visually complex photography unless a sufficiently dark overlay is present.

Interactive elements must have visible focus states.

Oxide red must not be the only indicator of state.

---

# 27. Logo Direction

The wordmark should feel simple, strong and institutional.

Preferred:

**CIVEP**

Uppercase geometric / grotesk wordmark.

Avoid:
- symbols built from flags
- globes
- dragons
- bridges
- arrows inside letters
- overly literal China–Europe icons

If a symbol is developed later, it should be abstract and architectural rather than illustrative.

---

# 28. Brand Recognition Devices

Primary recognition should come from:

1. Typography
2. Oxide red
3. Warm ivory
4. Industrial photography
5. Strong rules / grids
6. Numbered sections
7. Technical editorial labels

This allows the identity to become recognizable without relying on a decorative logo symbol.

# 30. Final Creative Standard

Every CIVEP page should pass the following test:

### Does it look credible next to a major strategy consultancy?

Yes.

### Does it still feel grounded in real industrial environments?

Yes.

### Could it be mistaken for a SaaS startup?

No.

### Could it be mistaken for a bank or private equity firm?

No.

### Does it communicate execution rather than theory?

Yes.

### Does the China–Europe positioning feel sophisticated rather than literal?

Yes.

---

# 31. Final Direction Summary

The CIVEP identity should be built around:

**Deep Ink + Warm Ivory + Oxide Red**

**Neue Haas Grotesk / Inter / Noto Sans SC**

**Large industrial photography**

**Editorial 12-column grids**

**Very limited border radius**

**Numbered service architecture**

**Technical labels and horizontal rules**

**Minimal motion**

**Large whitespace**

**No SaaS visual clichés**

The desired perception is:

> **A premium European industrial advisory firm with the commercial capability to help Chinese companies enter and grow in European markets.**
